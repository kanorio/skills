Kraut toastie, the loud way.

Two slices of bread, a lot of cheese and a forkful of Garlic Kraut. The kraut cuts through the cheese, and the pan does the rest.

MSMirela Santos
Head of recipes · 12 March 2026
Kraut goes
in the middle
Lid on for
2 minutes

You need

Method

  1. 1

    Squeeze the kraut. Hold it over the sink and press hard. Wet kraut makes a soggy toastie.

  2. 2

    Butter the outside. Every slice, edge to edge. Spread the mustard on the inside of two slices.

  3. 3

    Build it. Cheese, kraut, more cheese. The cheese on both sides is the glue.

  4. 4

    Cook it slowly. Medium heat, four minutes a side, with a lid for the first two so the cheese melts before the bread burns.

  5. 5

    Rest, then cut. One minute on the board, then corner to corner. Eat it standing up.

Tip

A teaspoon of brine in the hot pan just before the second side makes the crust tangier. Any more and it steams instead of crisps.

The kraut is the seasoning. You do not need anything else.

This is the recipe we make on packing days, when the crock room smells of garlic and nobody has time for lunch. It works with any of our krauts; the beet one turns the cheese pink, which is either a feature or a problem.

Salt, veg and time.

That is the whole recipe. The rest is patience, clay crocks and a crock room that smells of garlic most days.

Since 2019

Started in a borrowed restaurant kitchen with six crocks and no plan.

We got tired of fake sour.

Most pickles on the shelf are sour because of vinegar and safe because of heat. That works, but it flattens everything. Fermented vegetables get their sourness from the vegetables themselves, and they keep a crunch that vinegar never has.

So we do it the slow way. Vegetables, sea salt, a few spices, and three weeks in a crock at cellar temperature. No starter cultures, no shortcuts, no pasteurising the life out of it at the end.

It means smaller batches and the odd jar that sells out in a day. We think that is a fair trade.

Storage

Keeping
How long does a jar keep?

Six months unopened in the fridge, and a month once open. It keeps getting more sour, which some people like best.

Why is my jar fizzy?

Because it is alive. A little fizz and a cloudy brine are normal. Open it over the sink the first time.

Does it need the fridge?

Yes. Out of the fridge it ferments quickly and can push the lid up.
