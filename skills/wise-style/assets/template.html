What to keep, what to bin: receipts for sole traders

OHOwen Hartley
12 November 2026 · 6 min read

Most of the receipts in your wallet can go. The ones that prove a business cost cannot. Here is how to tell them apart in a few seconds.

The short versionKeep anything that shows what you bought, when, from whom and how much, if the cost was for the business. A clear photo usually counts as the receipt.

Keep anything that proves a business cost

A good receipt answers four questions: what was bought, on which date, from which supplier and for how much. Card statements show the last three but rarely the first, which is why the receipt still matters.

  • Supplier invoices and bills, paid or not.
  • Receipts for equipment, software, travel and materials.
  • Your own sales invoices, including cancelled ones.

How long to keep them

Rules differ by country, so check with your own tax office. Many ask for records to be kept for five to seven years after the return they support.

RecordKeep it?
Supplier invoicesYes, for the full period
Bank and card statementsYes, for the full period
Personal shoppingNo, unless part was for the business

Make it a five-minute habit

Snap each receipt the day you get it and forward email receipts to one address. Once a week, match what is left. Five minutes on a Thursday beats a lost Sunday in January.
