Story

From market stall to storefront in eighteen months

By Kenji Arata, small-business advisor / 7 min read

Marisol Fuentes sold fruit at the Saturday market for six years before she ever wrote down a price. "I priced by looking at the stall next to mine," she says. "If theirs said three, mine said two-fifty."

She came to a small-business hour because a supplier had raised her costs twice in one season, and the stall had stopped paying for itself.

The first hour: what does a mango really cost?

We started with one product. Buying price, the share that spoils, the van, the stall fee, her own time. The mango she sold for two-fifty cost her two-ninety.

I was paying people forty cents to take my best fruit home.

Over the next five sessions we built three things together: a price list with a margin on every line, a simple weekly ledger, and a plan for the slow winter months.

What helped most

  1. Writing every cost down for four weeks before changing a single price.
  2. Raising prices on the fruit people asked for by name, and nowhere else.
  3. Putting a fixed amount aside every Saturday night, before anything else.

Try this at home

Pick the thing you sell most. Add up everything it costs you for one month, including your hours, and divide by how many you sold. That is your floor.

Eighteen months after her first visit, Marisol signed a lease on a small storefront two streets from the market. The stall still runs on Saturdays.

Questions

Questions, answered plainly

Is it really free?

Yes. Every service is free, and we never sell or recommend products for a fee.

What should I bring to my first session?

Whatever you have: recent letters, a pay slip, bank statements. If you have nothing on paper, come anyway.

How do savings circles work?

Eight people each pick a goal and meet once a month with a coach. Nobody pools money; you save in your own account and keep each other on track.

Can I come with a family member?

Of course. Bring anyone who helps you feel comfortable.

Which languages do you offer?

English, Spanish, Tagalog and Vietnamese in person, and many more by phone interpreter.

Will you share my information?

No. We keep notes only to help you, and delete them when you ask. Read the privacy policy.
