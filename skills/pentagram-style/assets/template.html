    Harrow Botanic Garden

    One mark for a garden of nine glasshouses, in four seasonal colours.

    • Identities
    • Spaces and signs
    • Science
    Client
    Harrow Botanic Garden
    Year
    2026
    Partner
    Ines Corvo
    Scope
    Identity, signs, tickets, website

    The brief

    The garden had eleven logos, one for each glasshouse and shop, and visitors could not tell the café from the herbarium. It needed one name on the gate and one voice on every sign.

    The idea

    We drew one mark from four quarter circles, a seed seen from above, and let it change colour with the season. Signs, tickets and the website all switch on the first day of spring, summer, autumn and winter.

    About Quoin

    Quoin is an independent design studio with six partners and forty designers, writers and makers in Lisbon, Osaka and Toronto.

    We make identities, posters, packaging, books, typefaces, websites and the signs that help people find their way. Most projects need several of these at once, so most of our teams mix them too.

    Every project is led by a partner who designs it. The person you meet in the first conversation is the person who presents the work, and the one who answers the phone a year later.

    How we work

    Small teams, start to finish

    A project team is a partner, two to six designers and whoever the work needs: a writer, a type designer, a developer, a fabricator. It stays together until the work is in use.

    1. 01

      One partner, from the first call

      The partner who meets you designs the work and stays until it is in use.

    2. 02

      Make it early

      Real things are on the table in week two: a sign at full size, a jar, a spread. Opinions get better when there is something to hold.

    3. 03

      Leave rules behind

      Every project ends with guidelines short enough to read, and an afternoon with the people who will use them.

    Essay · 12 November 2026

    A logo is the smallest part of an identity

    What people remember is the voice, the rhythm and the way a place is signed. The mark is only where that starts.

    Written by Ines Corvo

    Every new client asks to see the logo first. We understand why: it is the part you can point at. But in our experience it does the least work of anything we make.

    What people actually meet

    A visitor to a botanic garden meets the ticket, the signs at the gate, the map, the menu in the café, the labels on two thousand plants and the voice on the website. The mark is on all of them, and it is rarely what anyone looks at.

    So we design the system first: a grid, a type family, a palette, a way of writing, and a short list of what changes and what stays. The mark comes out of that system, not the other way round.

    Design the voice first. The mark is the signature at the bottom of the letter.

    Rules people can keep

    An identity is only as good as the person setting a poster at five o'clock on a Friday. Our guidelines are short, they live in the tools people already use, and every rule comes with the reason for it.

    When a rule is broken often, we treat it as a design problem rather than a discipline problem, and redraw the rule.
