Today's margin

On leaving the last page of a notebook empty.

Ida Marsh

Every notebook I have finished
has one page I never wrote on.
It is always the last one.
I used to think this was carelessness,
the way you leave one biscuit in the tin
so that nobody has to admit it is gone.

Lately I think it is a kind of manners.
The last page is where the notebook
gets to say something back.
A smudge from the bag, a ring from a cup,
the faint print of every line I pressed too hard.
I read it like a postcard from the year.

This week a reader wrote to say
she keeps hers for the first line of the next book.
I liked that very much.
It means the notebooks hold hands.

Thank you for stopping by little hours today.
The kettle is on, as usual.

Ida

Part 2: The bowl that came back by post

23 November 2026 · Conversation with Ida Marsh · Photographs by Ruth Calder

Hana Brook makes about four hundred bowls a year in a shed behind her house, and every bowl leaves with a postcard asking the new owner to write back. Most do. In part one she told us how the postcards started. Today: the bowl that came home.

IdaYou said one bowl came back to you. In the post?

HanaIn a shoebox, wrapped in a tea towel. It had a crack right across the foot, and a letter folded inside it.

IdaWhat did the letter say?

HanaThat it had been her father's cereal bowl for eleven years. He had died in the spring, and she dropped it doing the washing up. She did not want a new one. She wanted to know if I could mend it.

IdaCould you?

HanaNot the way she hoped. You cannot put fired clay back in the kiln and make it whole. But I could fill the crack, and I could tell her honestly what it would be afterwards.

A mended bowl is a bowl for looking at,
not for porridge.

IdaDid she mind?

HanaShe wrote back to say that was exactly right. It lives on her windowsill now, and every winter she sends me a photograph of it with the frost behind.

IdaHas it changed how you make them?

HanaI sign the foot now, small. If one ever comes back, I will know it is mine before I open the box.

    Which size should I choose?

    A6 goes everywhere with you; A5 stays on the desk and has room for drawings and tickets.

    Can I start in the middle of the year?

    Of course. Every page is dated, so start on today's and leave the rest of the past alone.
