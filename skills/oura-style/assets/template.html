    How to run a bath that helps you wind down

    By Ines Calloway · 12 November · 4 min read

    Most of us run a bath the way we run a tap: hot, fast and while doing something else. A bath that helps you wind down asks for a little less heat and a little more attention.

    Four steps, ten minutes

    1. Cooler than you think. Aim for water that feels warm on the inside of your wrist, not hot.
    2. A handful, not a scoop. Two tablespoons of soak is plenty for a full bath.
    3. Lights low. One lamp in the next room, or a candle on the sill.
    4. Out before you are wrinkled. Ten to fifteen minutes, then balm while your skin is still warm.
    The point is not the bath. It is the ten minutes nobody asks anything of you.

    We make our soak with mineral salts, fine oat flour and dried chamomile from a grower up the coast. The oat softens the water; the salts are there because they feel good.

    A small studio on Saltmarsh Row

    Two of us, one long table and a kettle that is always on. We started making balm for our own hands in 2019 and never quite stopped.

    What we hold to

    Small batches

    Sixty jars at a time, so nothing sits on a shelf for months.

    Few ingredients

    If it does not earn its place, it does not go in.

    Close to home

    Jars, cups and boxes come from makers within a few miles.

    How a batch is made

    1. Melt

      Wax and butters go in the bain-marie at eight.

    2. Blend

      Oils and scent are weighed in by hand, never by eye.

    3. Pour

      Sixty jars, poured in one go while the balm is still clear.

    4. Date

      Each lid is stamped with the day it was poured.
