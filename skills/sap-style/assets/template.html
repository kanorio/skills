Questions buyers ask first

How long does it take to go live?

Most teams run their first module within six weeks. We import your suppliers and open contracts, connect your accounting system and train the people who approve.

Do we have to replace our accounting system?

No. Approved orders and matched invoices flow into the accounting system you already use, and payments stay where they are today.

What can the assistant do without us?

It can search, compare and draft. Anything that leaves the company, such as a quote request, an award or a renewal, waits for a person with the right permission.

Where is our data stored?

You choose the region when you sign up. Data stays in that region, and you can export everything at any time.

Can suppliers use it too?

Yes, free of charge. Suppliers answer quotes, upload documents and check payment status through a simple portal.

Marlow Pumps

Marlow Pumps cut sourcing cycles from 41 days to 12

A pump maker with three plants and a buying team of seven wanted to stop losing orders to slow quotes.

days from request to purchase order

The challenge

Quotes were requested by email from whichever suppliers a buyer remembered, copied into spreadsheets and compared by hand. One sourcing round took six weeks.

What changed

  • All 900 suppliers were imported with their categories, certificates and past prices.
  • Every request now becomes a sourcing event from a template, sent to a shortlist the assistant drafts.
  • Bids are compared on one screen, and awards go to the plant manager for approval in two clicks.

The result

Within a quarter, the average cycle fell to twelve days, and buyers spend their time negotiating instead of chasing.

We used to win the order and then lose the margin waiting for quotes. That gap is gone.

What three-way matching is

Before an invoice is paid, three documents are compared: the purchase order that says what you agreed to buy, the goods receipt that says what arrived, and the invoice that says what the supplier wants to be paid. When all three agree, the invoice can be paid without anyone opening it.

What to compare

FieldOrderReceiptInvoice
QuantityYesYesYes
Unit priceYesNoYes
Delivery dateYesYesNo
Tax and freightSometimesNoYes

Tolerances that keep the queue short

  • A price tolerance of one to two percent absorbs rounding and currency moves.
  • A quantity tolerance only where partial deliveries are normal, such as bulk materials.
  • No tolerance at all for new suppliers in their first three months.

Set tolerances by category, not by habit. Freight is not the same risk as packaging.

Amara Okoye

When a person should look

Send an invoice to a person when the price changed, when goods were short, when bank details changed recently, or when the supplier is new. Everything else can flow straight to payment on the agreed terms.
