        Inside the Saltglass sessions

        Nov.20.2026 · Words by Ines Aurel

        The record was made in one winter, in a rented glasshouse at the end of a sea wall, with one lamp, two microphones and a heater that worked on alternate days.

        Nadja Velour arrived with eleven songs and left with twelve. The last one, “Leave the Light On”, was written on the final night, when the storm took out the power and the band played it by torchlight into a single microphone.

        Salt on everything

        The glass walls let in the sound of the sea, and the engineer stopped trying to keep it out. You can hear the tide under “Undertow Waltz” and the rain on the roof at the end of “Pillar of Salt”.

        I wanted a record you could hear the weather on. Every song had to survive the room.

        Strings were recorded in one afternoon with eight players standing in a circle around the lamp. The arrangements were finished the night before, on the back of the call sheets.

                            About

                            Nadja Velour

                            A singer and songwriter who builds one world per record, then leaves it behind for the next.

                            She started with five songs on a laptop in a shared flat and a release at midnight with no warning. Six records later, every era still begins the same way: a room, a rule and a colour.

                            Gilded Debris was made for dance floors. Velvet Static lived inside late-night television. Paper Saints was written at a kitchen table. Hothouse Hymns moved into a palm house, and Saltglass into a glasshouse by the sea.

                            On stage, each era gets its own show, its own lettering and its own set. The Saltglass Procession opens in London in February 2027.
