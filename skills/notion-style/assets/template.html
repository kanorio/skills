Editing

The two-draft rule

Write the first draft fast and the second one calm. Mixing the two is why writing feels slow.

MEMara Ellison
March 4 · 6 min read

Most people edit while they write. They type a sentence, read it back, change a word, and start again. It feels careful. It is mostly slow.

The fix is a rule simple enough to remember on a busy day: two drafts, never at the same time.

Draft one is for getting it down. Draft two is for the reader. If you catch yourself fixing a comma in draft one, stop and keep going.

Draft one: fast and forgiving

Set a timer for fifteen minutes. Write everything you want to say in the order it comes to you. Leave gaps as [TK] and move on.

A messy draft you can see beats a perfect draft you are still imagining.

Draft two: calm and specific

Come back later, even ten minutes later. Read it as the person who will receive it, and ask three questions:

  • What do they need to know first?
  • What can I cut without losing meaning?
  • What should they do when they finish reading?
DraftGoalAllowed
OneGet it all downTypos, gaps, long sentences
TwoMake it easy to readCutting, reordering, rewriting

That is the whole rule. It takes a week to feel natural, and it saves time from the first day.
