Nine days in a church

How Static Cathedral was recorded live to tape, one room, no click track, every mistake kept.

Oct 9, 2026Words by Juno Varga6 min read

We booked the church for ten days and used nine. The tenth was for packing the van and sleeping on the floor of the vestry, which nobody admits to doing.

The rule was simple: the four of us in one room, no headphones, no click, and the first take that made the hair on Teo's arms stand up is the take on the record.

One room, four people

Stone walls give you about three seconds of reverb for free. That sounds like a gift until the snare comes back at you from the ceiling. We moved the kit four times before it sat right under the rose window.

The building played the twelfth instrument.

Rhys Calder, vocals

What we kept

  • The amp hum at the start of Nave. It was there every time, so it stayed.
  • A bell from the street on Ninth Day, two bars before the last chorus.
  • Every count-in, cut to one bar.

The band

Four people, one rehearsal room above a tire shop, five records and a lot of borrowed vans.

Loud on purpose

Cinderwall started as a dare between two school friends and a drum kit nobody could afford. The first shows were in a laundromat; the second year, they had a van.

Five records later the rule has not changed: everything is played live, in one room, by the four people on stage. If a song cannot survive that, it does not make the record.

The band plays about a hundred nights a year and answers its own mail. Booking, press and licensing go through the contact page.
