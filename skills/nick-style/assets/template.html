Cartoon Camp

Five days in a real studio, one finished cartoon to take home, and a premiere for families on Friday.

Book a week

How a week works

  1. Monday: ideas

    Everyone invents a character and gives it one big problem.

  2. Tuesday: storyboards

    Twelve boxes, one story. We pin them on the wall and act them out.

  3. Wednesday: drawing

    Frame by frame on light boxes. It's slow, and it's the best day.

  4. Thursday: sound

    Voices, footsteps and silly noises recorded in the booth.

  5. Friday: premiere

    Families come at 15:00 to watch every film on the big screen.

Twelve ways to draw a radish

Behind the scenes · 28 November · 4 min read

Before season two, the crew drew our hero a dozen ways. Here is how we picked one.

Every season starts with a wall of drawings. This time the wall had twelve radishes on it: tall ones, round ones, one with eyebrows and one that looked a lot like a beetroot.

We showed the wall to a class of seven-year-olds and asked one question: which radish would you trust with a rocket? They chose number eight, the one with goggles.

About the studio

Nine people, two light boxes per desk, and one small cinema upstairs.

Cartoons made by hand

Wobblebox started in 2014 in a spare room above a laundrette, with one pencil test and a lot of tea.

We still draw every frame by hand before it goes on screen. It takes longer, and the wobble in the lines is how you can tell a person made it.

Our shows are short, funny and kind, for kids from three to twelve. We test every episode with children before it is finished, and we change it when they are bored.

If a joke needs explaining to a six-year-old, we cut it.

For grown-ups

The questions parents, carers and teachers ask us most.

What ages are your shows for?

Each show has an age on its page: three to six, six to nine, or nine to twelve. They're a guide, not a rule. Plenty of grown-ups watch the radish.

Are the screenings loud or dark?

No. Sound is kept low, the lights stay half up and you can come and go. The only exception is the night-light screening, which says so on its card.

Do I stay with my child at a screening?

Yes. Children under twelve need a grown-up in the room at screenings. At workshops and Cartoon Camp you drop off and collect.

What happens at Cartoon Camp if it rains?

Nothing changes. Camp happens indoors in the studio, with a walk to the park at lunch when the weather allows.

Can my child's school visit the studio?

Yes, on Wednesday mornings during term, one class at a time. Ask us about a date.

Where can we watch the shows at home?

Each show page says where its episodes are available. New episodes always play in the Picture House first.

Still wondering?

Send us a note and a real person from the studio answers, usually the same day.

What we collect

When you book a seat, a camp place or send a message, we keep the names, email address and notes you give us. We use them to answer you and run the booking, and for nothing else.

Children

Children don't create accounts on this site. Bookings for children are always made by a grown-up, and we only ask for a camper's first name and age.

How long we keep it

Booking details are deleted twelve months after the event. Newsletter addresses stay until you unsubscribe, which you can do from any email.

Tickets and refunds

Screening tickets can be moved to another date up to a day before. Camp places are refunded in full until 1 June.
