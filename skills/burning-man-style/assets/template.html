The Essentials

When

Gates open Thursday, October 7 at 6 pm. The last night is Wednesday, October 13; the land is clear by Friday noon.

Where

Halite Flats, forty miles of graded road past Corran Valley. Exact directions come with your pass.

Passes

A sliding scale from $90 to $260 for the week. One pass in ten is set aside at $40 for anyone who asks.

Bring

Water for a week, shade, warm layers for the nights, a light for your bike and bags to carry out everything you brought.

Getting There

  • Drive yourself

    Fill up in Corran Valley; there is no fuel on the flats. The last forty miles are graded gravel, slow but fine for any car. Carpools of three or more get a parking spot by the gate.

  • Take the shuttle

    Buses leave Corran Valley station every two hours from noon on opening day. Seats are booked with your pass, and each rider gets one large bag and one cooler.

  • Share a ride

    The ride board opens in August. Post your seats or your request and settle fuel costs between yourselves.

Before you pack

Leave No Trace, Not Even a Sequin

  • Everything you bring leaves with you, including grey water and food scraps.
  • No feathers, glitter or loose decorations; the wind takes them for miles.
  • Fires burn only in raised bowls on the fire field, with a safety crew on watch.

Art & Program/Installation

Signal Tower

A fifty-foot lattice that glows from the inside and hums when the wind picks up. Walk under it, and the lamps above you brighten one level at a time.

The Long Table crew spent two winters cutting the lattice from steel salvaged from a closed grain mill. Each of the sixteen levels holds ninety lamps on its own circuit, so the tower can breathe: brightest at the base after dusk, brightest at the crown just before dawn.

On the last night the lamps go out one level at a time while everyone stands in the dark together. Nothing is burned; the tower is taken down by the same crew and stored for the next town that asks for it.

We wanted something you could find from anywhere on the flats, and then stand inside of.

Ines Okafor, lead artist

Volunteer a Shift

Most shifts are four hours. Training happens on site or at an Ember Night before the week.

  • Gate and greeters

    Check passes, hand out maps, welcome people home.

  • Build crew

    Raise frames and wire lamps in the two weeks before gates open.

  • Fire safety

    Watch the fire field with a trained lead. Level one class required.

  • Morning sweep

    Walk a line at dawn and pick up anything that is not dust.

What Two Thousand Paper Lanterns Taught Us About Patience

The Lantern Choir took eleven months, four hundred volunteers and one very long table. Its artist on folding, failing and the night it finally sang.

MCMira Castellane
November 18, 2026 · 7 minute read

The first lantern took me forty minutes. The paper tore twice, the wire bent the wrong way, and when I finally switched it on, it looked like a lunch bag with a cold inside. The two-thousandth took four minutes, and I folded it with my eyes half closed at an Ember Night in Eastport, surrounded by people I had met that evening.

A shape anyone can fold

The rule from the start was that anyone should be able to make one. No glue, no tools beyond a bone folder, nine folds you can learn by watching someone else's hands. That rule cost us the pretty version I had drawn, and it is the reason the choir exists at all.

Every lantern on that field was folded by somebody who could tell you where they were sitting when they made it.

Mira Castellane

What went wrong

  • Our first batch of stems rusted in a week of rain, so we switched to powder-coated steel.
  • The dimmers whined. We rewired every row so the hum sits below the wind.
  • Wind on the flats is not a breeze; each stem now sits forty centimetres into the ground.

The night it sang

On the Saturday, we turned the lanterns on row by row as people walked in, so the light followed them. Nobody planned the singing. Somebody started humming near the middle and the field picked it up. We have kept the name.

Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers about the week, passes, art and safety. Still stuck? Write to us and a person will answer.

The Week

Is it a music festival?

No. There are no headliners and no stages run by us. Music happens because people bring it, mostly at night and mostly in the south arc.

Can I come for one night?

Yes. Every pass covers the whole week, so come for as long as you like. Arrivals close at midnight each night.

Are children welcome?

Yes, with a parent or guardian at all times. Under twelve enter free, and the family camp sits away from the late sound.

Passes

How does the sliding scale work?

Choose what you can afford between $90 and $260. Paying more funds the low-income passes; nobody checks why you chose a price.

Can I transfer my pass?

Yes, until September 30, through your pass page. Resale above face value cancels the pass.

Art and Volunteering

Do I need experience to apply for a grant?

No. First-time builders are paired with a mentor crew, and half of last year's grants went to people who had never built anything this size.

Do volunteers still need a pass?

Volunteers who work three shifts or more get their pass refunded at the end of the week.

Safety and Leave No Trace

Where can I have a fire?

Only in raised bowls on the fire field, with the safety crew present. Camp stoves are fine; open fires in camp are not.

Is there medical help on site?

A first aid dome is open day and night, staffed by licensed volunteers, with an ambulance on call from Corran Valley.

What happens to rubbish?

You take it home. There are no bins on the flats, on purpose. The morning sweep checks every campsite before the gate closes.

About us

A Collective That Builds in the Open

We started as forty friends with a generator and a borrowed truck. We are still mostly volunteers.

What We Do

The collective funds art that anyone can walk up to, runs one week on Halite Flats, and helps volunteers host Ember Nights in their own towns. Then we leave the land as we found it.

How We Work

  1. Everyone builds

    There is no audience. If you are here, you are part of it.

  2. Gifts, not sales

    Nothing is sold on the flats except ice and coffee for the crews.

  3. Leave no trace

    We carry out everything we carry in, and we check.

  4. Look after each other

    Safety crews, first aid and quiet spaces are never cut from the budget.

Privacy

We collect what you give us in our forms: your name, your email and anything you write. We use it to answer you, to run volunteer shifts and to send the newsletter you asked for. We never sell it.

You can ask to see, correct or delete your information at any time by writing to hello@emberwell.example.

Terms
