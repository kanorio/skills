Soft Machines

Furniture that folds, rocks and rolls: ninety objects that change shape to fit the way we live, from a 1920s steamer chair to a sofa that becomes a bed in one pull.

Where
Gallery 2, Level 1
Tickets
$18, members free
Allow
About 75 minutes
Access
Step-free, seats in every room
Book tickets

Rocking chair for a small flat, 1964. Bent beech and canvas. Loaned by the Halvorsen family.

Three ways to move

Fold

Chairs, tables and screens that flatten against a wall, made for rooms that have to be three rooms a day.

Rock

Curved runners and sprung seats, from nursery chairs to a recliner built for a long-haul cockpit.

Roll

Cabinets, desks and beds on castors that let one floor plan change with the week.

Getting here

  • By ferryPier stop, then a two-minute walk
  • By busRoutes 7 and 12 to Ferry Pier Road
  • By bikeRacks for 80 bikes at the north door
  • By carNo visitor parking; Harbour Square car park is 400 m away

Step-free

Every floor by lift and ramp. Wheelchairs and folding stools to borrow at the desk.

Quiet mornings

First Sunday of the month, 9:00 to 10:00, with lower lights and sound.

Captions on

Every film and talk is captioned. Ask for a hearing loop at any desk.

Questions

Can I bring a bag or a pram?

Yes. Bags larger than a cabin case go in the free lockers on Level 0. Prams are welcome in every gallery.

Can I take photos?

Photos without flash are fine for personal use, except where a sign in the room says otherwise.

Is there somewhere to eat?

The Pier Café on Level 0 serves lunch and coffee from 10:00. You can also eat your own food on the terrace.

Do I need to book in advance?

Not for the galleries. Workshops and films have limited places, so book those ahead.

Can schools and groups visit?

Yes, with free entry for school groups on weekdays. Write to us two weeks before you come.

About Obra

A public museum for the things people design and the images they make, in a former ferry shed at the end of the pier.

The building

The old ferry shed was rebuilt in 2019 with three floors of galleries, a 220-seat cinema and a terrace over the water.

The collection

Chairs, posters, packaging, type, film and games, collected because they changed how ordinary days look and feel.

The programme

Four exhibitions a year, films every night but Monday, and workshops where you make something to take home.
