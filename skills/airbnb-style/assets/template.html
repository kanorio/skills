A small A-frame on a bend of the Wren, ten minutes on foot from the river path. Downstairs is one warm room: a kitchen with a long oak table, a sofa that faces the stove, and a glass wall onto the pines. Upstairs, under the roof, are two bedrooms with linen bedding and wool blankets. The wood-fired sauna sits on the bank, with a ladder into a slow, deep pool. Mirela lives in the farmhouse up the lane and leaves bread, eggs and a hand-drawn map of the walks on the table.

A weekend on the river path

J
Jonah Prior

March 2 · 6 min read

The river path runs eleven kilometres from Fernley to the old mill, and nobody walks all of it in one go. You don't need to. Here is how we spend two slow days on it.

Saturday: the lower bend

Start at the station and follow the water upstream. After an hour the path drops to a gravel beach where the river slows and turns green. Swim if the sun is out, then walk up through the pines to the cabin.

Bring less than you think. The valley has everything else.

Sunday: the mill and back

  • Leave early: the mist sits on the water until nine.
  • The mill café opens at ten and sells out of buns by noon.
  • Take the high path home for the view over the coast.

Back at the cabin, light the sauna before you leave in the morning and it will be ready when you return.

One letter a month

New places, open dates and a walk worth taking. No more than that.

You're on the list. The next letter goes out on the first of the month.

Most places have a lockbox. The code arrives by email the morning of your check-in.

Are pets welcome?

In some places. Use the Pets welcome filter, and tell your host what kind of pet is coming.

What does hosting cost?

Listing is free. We keep a share of each booking to pay for cleaning, photos and guest support.

Do I have to live nearby?

No, but someone has to be within half an hour of the place in case a guest needs help.
